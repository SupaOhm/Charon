# Myth and visual notes, art revision 2

Prepared September 27, 2026. These are prototype art adaptations, not a single canonical map of the Greek underworld.

## Environment design

| Area | Literary basis | Visual interpretation delivered |
|---|---|---|
| Starting shore | Homer, Odyssey 10.509-515: Persephone's poplars and willows, rivers meeting beside a rock. [Translation](https://www.theoi.com/Text/HomerOdyssey10.html) | Leaning willow, pale poplar grove, waiting shades, dark confluence and rough boarding plank. The embarkation layout is adapted for this game. |
| Elysium | Homer, Odyssey 4.561-569: mild Elysian plain and Ocean's west wind. [Translation](https://www.theoi.com/Text/HomerOdyssey4.html). Pindar, Olympian 2, approximately 68-80: blessed island, ocean breezes and golden flowers. [Translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Odes_of_Pindar_(Paley)/Olympian_Ode_2) | Open flowering coast, sunlit grass, sea and distant islands. Combining these literary variants is an art choice. It does not assign moral worth to game passengers. |
| Asphodel | Homer, Odyssey 24.1-14: shades reach the asphodel meadow. [Translation](https://www.theoi.com/Text/HomerOdyssey24.html) | An extensive flat flower field with distant shades. Lavender fog and the worn path are atmospheric choices. |
| Tartarus | Hesiod, Theogony, approximately 715-744 and 807-819: deep gulf, bronze enclosure and gates, with Titans beyond. [Translation](https://www.theoi.com/Text/HesiodTheogony.html) | A steep underground abyss, immense bronze gates and enclosing barrier. Violet mist and small ember fissures are invented lighting choices, not claims about a literal lava landscape. |
| Haven | Original maintenance stop in the game, not a named ancient myth location. | A close timber workshop inside a sheltered rock alcove, boat ribs, rope, spare planks and warm lamplight. |

Each scene now has its own terrain, spatial scale and dominant silhouette. Avoid returning all destinations to a central river between similar cliffs. Shared painted grain and recurring lantern materials connect the set.

## Wraith

The wraith is a game spirit, not a named Erinys or a claimed ancient depiction. The revision uses a furious face, burning eyes, reaching fingers, a twisted posture and torn shroud. These replace the first pass's passive, sorrowful pose. Threat is conveyed through expression and silhouette without gore.

## Currency and play elements

- **Obol:** cool hammered silver, irregular rim, original oar-and-wave emblem. A Greek Attic silver obol in the Metropolitan Museum provides a material reference, not the emblem or an image source: [Silver obol, accession 2021.40.82](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/848360). This is not an archaeological replica. No claim is made that all Greek burials used the same payment custom.
- **Light:** a warm amber flame within the guide's bronze lantern. Its upright shape differs from the round silver coin.
- **Hull:** boat ribs and hull. **Reprimand:** an outlined warning seal. **Fog:** drifting horizontal lines. **Rock:** jagged peaks. **Anger:** a sharp-edged scowling mark. **Protection:** a shield and check.
- **Relationships:** interlocking links for a pair; crossed weapons for conflict. Adjacent labels remain necessary.
- **Destinations:** willow, flower, asphodel stem, descending gate and workshop outlines echo the environments.
- **Memories:** a shield with crossed oars, watchful eye, recollection spiral, reconciled weapons, joined circles and faint spark distinguish the six existing memory types. Exact effects remain live rule text.
- **Cards and controls:** a shared painted memory back, blank soul/memory/route frames, resource chips and action styles. Numeric counters are HTML text. The common memory back does not reveal an effect or encode a new mechanic.
